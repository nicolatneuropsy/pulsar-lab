Our research extends from the brain oscillations that support timing, attention and working memory to the clinical tools that measure these functions. It is organized around three axes.

{{< axe numero="1" titre="Time perception" >}}

How do attention and working memory shape our perception of duration, and do they act the same way below and above about 1.2 seconds?

**Methods:** behavioral tasks, and electrophysiology (EEG, SEEG) in some projects. **Participants:** mostly adults from the general population.

{{< projet titre="TIC-TAC" >}}
Participants reproduce the duration of a tone while holding colors in working memory, under different priorities between the two tasks and different memory loads. The study compares three current accounts of what attention protects when it protects duration, and asks whether the 1.2-second boundary also separates two kinds of attention.
{{< /projet >}}

{{< projet titre="Passive perception and reproduction of sub-second and supra-second intervals: an SEEG study" >}}
This study takes up the approach of Nicola Thibault’s doctoral research, which used EEG and a passive deviance detection paradigm to show that intervals below and above 1.2 seconds rely on distinct brain mechanisms. With intracranial recordings (stereo-EEG), it seeks to classify the oscillations that support the passive perception and the reproduction of these intervals.

In collaboration with Philippe Albouy and Simon Grondin (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}

{{< axe numero="2" titre="Executive and cognitive functions" >}}

This axis is in development. It examines how brain oscillations support working memory and executive control.

**Methods:** high-density EEG, with source reconstruction from each participant’s MRI, and transcranial magnetic stimulation guided by neuronavigation. **Participants:** healthy young adults.

{{< projet titre="AI-assisted decision making: electrophysiological mechanisms" >}}
Participants hear everyday dilemmas and receive the same advice, attributed either to an artificial intelligence or to a human expert. High-density EEG measures whether advice attributed to an AI reduces the engagement of the central executive network, a sign of cognitive offloading.

A project of the Canada Excellence Research Chair in Neuroplasticity (Steven Laureys, Université Laval), with Roxane Hoyer.
{{< /projet >}}

{{< projet titre="Transcranial magnetic stimulation and visuospatial working memory" >}}
This study tests whether rhythmic transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) of the parietal cortex at theta frequency improves visuospatial working memory compared with a sham condition, while EEG records the brain’s response. It uses the task that the third axis is validating for clinical use.

In collaboration with Roxane Hoyer (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}

{{< axe numero="3" titre="Neuropsychology: developing and validating open-access tasks" >}}

This axis aims to give neuropsychologists in Québec open-access tasks, validated and normed in the Québec population.

**Methods:** computerized tasks, psychometric validation and norming. **Participants:** clinical and nonclinical populations.

{{< projet titre="Validation of a neuropsychological visuospatial working memory task" >}}
Common clinical tests of visuospatial working memory, such as spatial span and Corsi blocks, combine the retention of locations and their mental manipulation in a single score. Our computerized task separates the two. Developed for research with Professor Roxane Hoyer at CERVO, it is being adapted for clinical use and validated in the general population before norms are established. Future projects will also address criterion validity for various conditions, such as mild traumatic brain injury.

In collaboration with Roxane Hoyer (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}
