Our research extends from the brain oscillations that support timing, attention and working memory to the clinical tools that measure these functions. It is organized around three axes.

{{< axe numero="1" titre="Time perception" >}}

How do attention and working memory shape our perception of duration, and do they act the same way below and above about 1.2 seconds?

**Methods:** behavioral tasks, and electrophysiology (EEG, SEEG) in some projects. **Participants:** mostly adults from the general population.

{{< projet titre="TIC-TAC" >}}
What does attention protect when it protects the perception of duration?

- Do ignoring time and overloading working memory impair duration judgments in the same way?
- How does a brief distraction disrupt perceived duration: through its similarity to the timed signal, through the expectation of an interruption, or because the internal clock is not consulted?
- Does the boundary of about 1.2 seconds also separate two kinds of attention?
- Which measures of interference are reliable enough to study differences between individuals?
{{< /projet >}}

{{< projet titre="Passive perception and reproduction of sub-second and supra-second intervals: an SEEG study" >}}
Which brain oscillations support the passive perception and the reproduction of intervals shorter and longer than one second?

In collaboration with Philippe Albouy and Simon Grondin (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}

{{< axe numero="2" titre="Executive and cognitive functions" >}}

This axis is in development. It examines how brain oscillations support working memory and executive control.

**Methods:** high-density EEG, with source reconstruction from each participant’s MRI, and transcranial magnetic stimulation guided by neuronavigation. **Participants:** healthy young adults.

{{< projet titre="AI-assisted decision making: electrophysiological mechanisms" >}}
Does attributing advice to an artificial intelligence rather than to a human expert reduce the mental effort people invest in everyday decisions?

- Is the brain’s central executive network less engaged when advice comes from an AI?
- Do people follow advice attributed to an AI more often, and more quickly?
- Do each person’s attitudes toward AI and decision style modulate this effect?

In collaboration with Steven Laureys and Roxane Hoyer (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}

{{< projet titre="Transcranial magnetic stimulation and visuospatial working memory" >}}
Do theta oscillations play a causal role in visuospatial working memory?

- Does theta-rhythm stimulation of the parietal cortex improve the retention and the manipulation of spatial information?
- Is the effect stronger when information must be manipulated rather than retained?
- Does the response to stimulation vary from one person to another, for example with spontaneous theta frequency, executive abilities or anxiety?

In collaboration with Roxane Hoyer (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}

{{< axe numero="3" titre="Neuropsychology: developing and validating open-access tasks" >}}

This axis aims to give neuropsychologists in Québec open-access tasks, validated and normed in the Québec population.

**Methods:** computerized tasks, psychometric validation and norming. **Participants:** clinical and nonclinical populations.

{{< projet titre="Validation of a neuropsychological visuospatial working memory task" >}}
Can a computerized task that measures the retention of locations and their mental manipulation separately become a valid, normed clinical tool for Québec?

Future projects will also address criterion validity for various conditions, such as mild traumatic brain injury.

In collaboration with Roxane Hoyer (Université Laval).
{{< /projet >}}
